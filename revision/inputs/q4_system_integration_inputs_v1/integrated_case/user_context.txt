[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background: We are developing multicomponent Mo/Ni chalcogenide microspheres with internal nanovoids as a sodium-ion battery anode. The original design problem focused on particle architecture and scalable processing; this extension treats the particle, electrolyte, and SEI as one coupled system.
- Particle bottleneck: Sodium storage causes large strain and loss of contact. Internal voids and a conductive framework can buffer deformation, but excessive void space, shell thickness, or inactive carbon lowers packing density and practical utilization.
- Process bottleneck: Spray drying and post-treatment must retain uniform active-phase distribution and a mechanically coherent secondary particle rather than creating fragile shells, disconnected domains, or collapsed porosity.
- Electrolyte bottleneck: Solvation and desolvation control sodium-ion delivery to the particle, while solvent, salt, and additive decomposition determine the chemistry and transport resistance of the SEI.
- Dynamic-interphase bottleneck: Repeated particle expansion and contraction can fracture the SEI, expose fresh surface, consume electrolyte and sodium inventory, and generate a progressively thicker or heterogeneous interphase. Physical voids alone do not prevent this feedback loop.
- Coupling requirement: The particle architecture must limit local stress and fresh-surface generation while the electrolyte must form an electronically blocking, ionically conductive, mechanically tolerant, and renewable interphase without unacceptable viscosity, gas evolution, or inactive mass.
- Core question: What integrated particle-electrolyte-SEI hypothesis best breaks the feedback among volume change, interphase fracture, parasitic electrolyte consumption, transport loss, and particle isolation while retaining scalable powder processing and practical electrode density?
- Goals:
    1) Specify particle-level structural variables and electrolyte/interphase variables in the same causal hypothesis.
    2) Explain why the selected electrolyte response should remain compatible with the proposed particle strain and surface-area evolution.
    3) Define a minimal factorial validation separating architectural buffering from electrolyte/SEI stabilization and testing their interaction.
