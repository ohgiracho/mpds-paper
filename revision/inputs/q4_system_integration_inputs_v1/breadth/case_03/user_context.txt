[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background: Sodium-metal reversibility is governed by a nanoscale, chemically heterogeneous SEI that controls ion transport, deposition morphology, and irreversible loss.
- Dynamic bottleneck: Large plating/stripping volume fluctuations repeatedly fracture, dissolve, and reconstruct the interphase, so a static post-cycling composition is not sufficient to describe function.
- Measurement trade-off: Ex situ disassembly, rinsing, and vacuum transfer can perturb the fragile interphase. Operando methods preserve the interface but often provide indirect contrast or insufficient chemical specificity for individual inorganic phases.
- Protocol dependence: Formation conditions may determine whether a mechanically and ionically functional inorganic framework develops, but its evolution cannot be inferred from end-point images alone.
- Core question: What chemically specific, minimally perturbative measurement and control strategy can link formation protocol, phase-resolved SEI evolution, and sodium deposition reversibility?
- Goals:
    1) Resolve interphase evolution over time rather than a single static state.
    2) Separate the behavior of a targeted inorganic phase from total interphase thickness or morphology.
    3) Define a protocol-dependent prediction that can be falsified during cycling.
