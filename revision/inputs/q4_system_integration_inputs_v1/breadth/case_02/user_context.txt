[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background: Lithium-metal batteries require stable electrode-electrolyte interphases under aggressive high-energy operating conditions.
- Dynamic-interface bottleneck: The interphase repeatedly reconstructs because electrolyte components continuously adsorb, decompose, and react at the electrodes.
- Multi-component trade-off: Practical formulations require several additives, but their functions are not independent. One component can alter another component's adsorption, local chemical environment, and decomposition pathway.
- Knowledge gap: Solvation-structure descriptions alone do not predict competitive interfacial reactions or the final interphase chemistry.
- Core question: How can additive roles be selected and combined using a mechanism-based rule that anticipates competitive adsorption and decomposition rather than empirical screening alone?
- Goals:
    1) Propose a testable model of additive-additive and additive-interface competition.
    2) Define how the formulation should be screened without assuming that single-additive behavior is preserved in a mixture.
    3) Identify measurements that distinguish adsorption, decomposition order, and interphase reconstruction.
