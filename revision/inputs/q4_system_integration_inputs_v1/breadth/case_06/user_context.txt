[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background: Anode-free lithium-metal batteries remove excess lithium and can improve energy density and manufacturing practicality, but zero lithium excess makes every irreversible loss critical.
- Inventory bottleneck: Electrolyte depletion and an insufficient active-lithium reservoir amplify capacity loss from each cycle.
- Morphology bottleneck: Local current concentration and nonuniform deposition generate electronically disconnected dead lithium.
- Interphase bottleneck: Slow lithium-ion transfer through the SEI and continued electrolyte decomposition reinforce dead-lithium growth.
- Strategy trade-off: Extra lithium sources reduce the anode-free advantage, while isolated electrolyte or current-collector modifications may not coordinate dead-metal recovery, electron blocking, and ion conduction.
- Core question: What coupled collector-electrolyte-SEI mechanism can prevent or reverse dead-lithium accumulation without adding a large sacrificial lithium inventory?
- Goals:
    1) Manage lithium and electrolyte inventories explicitly.
    2) Combine uniform deposition, electronic passivation, and rapid ionic transport.
    3) Propose diagnostics that distinguish prevention of dead lithium from reactivation of previously isolated lithium.
