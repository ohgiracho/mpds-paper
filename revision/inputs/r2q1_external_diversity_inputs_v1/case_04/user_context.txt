[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background: Repeated ion insertion and extraction couples electrochemical reactions to mechanical stress and cumulative fatigue in layered oxide cathodes.
- Unresolved observation: Eliminating grain boundaries does not eliminate capacity decay; nominally single-crystalline particles still develop planar intragranular cracks during high-voltage cycling.
- Structural clues: Cracks tend to propagate along crystallographic basal planes and become prominent in highly delithiated states where transition-metal slabs glide and different layer stackings interconvert.
- Knowledge gap: Conventional phase-transition descriptions do not fully explain where intragranular cracks nucleate, why damage accumulates, or which synthesis-stage defect population controls susceptibility.
- Core question: Which pre-existing or cycling-induced crystallographic defect could couple slab gliding, oxygen instability, transition-metal rearrangement, and planar crack formation?
- Goals:
    1) Propose a defect-to-reaction-to-crack causal chain with distinguishable predictions.
    2) Identify a synthesis or thermal-processing control that changes the suspected defect without changing bulk composition.
    3) Specify atomic-scale, operando, and cycling controls that could falsify the mechanism.
