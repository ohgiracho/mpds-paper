[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background: The cathode is a major determinant of lithium-ion-cell energy density and cost. High-nickel, low-cobalt layered oxides improve capacity and reduce cobalt use, but they remain exposed to nickel price, supply-chain, environmental, and processing burdens.
- Materials bottleneck: Increasing nickel commonly lowers thermal stability and accelerates structural degradation and cycling failure, whereas simply lowering nickel can sacrifice voltage and capacity.
- Sustainability and manufacturing constraints: The design should reduce or eliminate cobalt, lower nickel dependence, avoid unusually expensive precursors or processing atmospheres, and remain compatible with scalable cathode manufacture.
- Core trade-off: Composition changes that reduce critical-material content must not destabilize the layered framework, increase cation disorder, or shift failure into another structural or interfacial mode.
- Core question: What composition-and-processing hypothesis could jointly preserve energy density, rate capability, and long-term structural stability in a low-nickel, cobalt-free layered cathode?
- Goals:
    1) Propose a mechanistic composition design rather than a list of dopants.
    2) Explain how the design balances redox activity, lattice stability, transport, and cost.
    3) Specify synthesis controls and decisive structural, thermal, and electrochemical tests that could falsify the mechanism.
