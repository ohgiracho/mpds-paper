[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background:
 We are developing a sodium-ion anode using a PBA-derived transition-metal sulfide combined with conductive carbon. The material pairing is fixed, but the particle morphology, carbon organization, component arrangement, and synthesis route remain open design variables.

- Main bottleneck:
 Repeated conversion reactions and sodium-driven volume change can fracture the active phase, disrupt conductive contact, and accelerate capacity loss. A stabilizing architecture is needed without sacrificing active-material fraction or electrode density.

- Transport bottleneck:
 The composite must provide continuous electron transport and electrolyte access to the sulfide while avoiding excessive carbon dilution, blocked ion pathways, or long diffusion distances.

- Structural bottleneck:
 The active phase and conductive framework must remain coupled during cycling. Aggregation, detached fragments, framework collapse, and loss of interparticle contact are unacceptable failure modes.

- Process concern:
 The manufacturing route should produce a reproducible composite with practical particle handling and scalable throughput. The target morphology and target-producing process are not specified in advance.

- Evaluation task:
 Compare plausible particle architectures, carbon-integration strategies, and scalable synthesis routes. Select a preferred design based on transport, strain accommodation, density, and process robustness; state the main trade-offs; and propose measurements that could distinguish the preferred mechanism from alternatives.
