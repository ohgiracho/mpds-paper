A standard, undoped carbon surface is non-polar and has a weak affinity for polar polysulfides/polyselenides. This is likely the primary reason for your active species loss. Research has conclusively shown that heteroatom doping, particularly with nitrogen, creates polar sites on the carbon surface that chemically adsorb polysulfides, drastically improving retention [ID: 10, 171]. DFT calculations and X-ray absorption spectroscopy have confirmed this mechanism, moving it from hypothesis to established fact [ID: 10]. Boron doping has a similar effect [ID: 19]. Without this chemical anchor, you are fighting a losing battle against dissolution.

3.  **Conductivity and Structural Integrity are Coupled:** Your concern about the carbon framework separating into "poorly wired domains" is astute. This is a common failure mode. A collection of nanofibers, even if individually conductive, may lose contact with each other and the current collector due to the significant volume changes during Se ↔ Li₂Se conversion. MOF-derived carbons, while porous, are often not highly graphitic and can have limited intrinsic conductivity [ID: 106]. Relying on particle-to-particle contact in a pressed electrode is unreliable. A truly robust electrode requires a continuous, three-dimensional conductive network that is mechanically resilient [ID: 11, 47].

This architecture effectively decouples the different functions: the N-doped nanofibers provide high-surface-area, chemically active sites for Se loading and confinement, while the graphene provides the long-range conductivity and structural backbone.
