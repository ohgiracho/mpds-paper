**Subject:** Decision Memorandum on LPSCl Geometric Allocation in High-Loading NCM811 Cathodes

### 1. Synthesis of Arguments

The debate between Scientist A (Architect) and Scientist B (Engineer) centered on the optimal geometric allocation of a limited amount of LPSCl solid electrolyte (SE) within a mechanofused, high-loading (>85 wt%) polycrystalline NCM811 composite cathode. The goal is to balance ionic continuity, electronic accessibility, and packing density.

Both debaters correctly identified that a simplistic, monolithic allocation of LPSCl—either as a pure surface coating or as a pure interstitial filler—is fundamentally flawed. Scientist A argued that a pure coating would isolate the electronic network [ID: 421, 463], while pure interstitial filling would create "ion-starved" active material particles with poor rate capability [ID: 31, 157]. Scientist B concurred, emphasizing the competing demands within the constrained cathode volume [ID: 2, 48].

The consensus strategy that emerged was the use of a **hierarchical architecture employing a bimodal particle size distribution of LPSCl**. Both Scientist A [ID: 345] and Scientist B [ID: 70] referenced work showing that mixed-sized electrolytes improve electrochemical performance. The proposed roles are:
*   **Fine LPSCl Fraction:** To be used as a coating on the NCM811 particles to maximize the active interfacial area and reduce local ionic diffusion limitations [ID: 31, 252].
*   **Coarse LPSCl Fraction:** To be used as an interstitial filler to increase packing density, provide mechanical stability, and form a low-tortuosity "ionic highway" for efficient long-range transport through the thick electrode [ID: 157, 5].

The primary point of contention was the fabrication method. Scientist A ultimately diverged from the project constraints, advocating for liquid-phase infiltration and single-crystal NCM [ID: 11, 152], which are outside the scope of our current fixed platform. Scientist B rightly critiqued this approach for its process complexity and the fundamental incompatibility of most solvents with sulfide electrolytes [ID: 23, 96].

Scientist B’s final proposal, a **Hierarchically-Structured, Dry-Film Composite**, is more aligned with our constraints. It leverages mechanofusion as a strategic tool for a specific task—dry-coating the NCM811 with fine LPSCl [ID: 411]—before blending with coarser LPSCl for the bulk structure. This solvent-free approach directly addresses the prompt while respecting the material and process limitations we face.

### 2. Director's Assessment and Recommended Path Forward

Scientist A’s arguments regarding alternative platforms are noted for future R&D, but they are not actionable for the current optimization task. Scientist B’s final proposed strategy is the most pragmatic, scientifically sound, and compliant with our project constraints.

Therefore, the recommended path forward is to engineer a hierarchical composite by strategically allocating LPSCl based on particle size, using our existing mechanofusion process as a key fabrication tool.

**The Recommended Strategy:**

1.  **Bimodal LPSCl Preparation:** The total LPSCl budget will be divided into two fractions: a fine, sub-micron powder and a coarser, micron-sized powder.
2.  **Interfacial Engineering (Surface Coverage):** Employ a controlled, lower-energy mechanofusion process to dry-coat the polycrystalline NCM811 secondary particles with the **fine LPSCl fraction**. The goal is to form NCM@LPSCl core-shell particles, establishing an intimate, uniform ionic interface directly on the active material surface [ID: 411, 31]. This step allocates a portion of the SE to maximize ionic accessibility at the source.
3.  **Bulk Structuring (Interstitial Filling):** The resulting NCM@LPSCl core-shell particles will then be blended in a second, lower-energy mixing step with the **coarse LPSCl fraction** and the existing conductive additive. The coarse SE will fill the interstitial voids between the core-shell particles, ensuring high packing density and creating a continuous, low-tortuosity ionic network [ID: 2, 70].
4.  **Solvent-Free Densification:** The final composite powder will be densified into an electrode via calendering or cold pressing. This entirely solvent-free route is critical for preserving the integrity of the LPSCl [ID: 23, 50].

This approach directly answers the central question: the limited LPSCl is allocated by function. Fine particles are dedicated to surface coverage for kinetics, while coarse particles are dedicated to interstitial filling for density and long-range transport. This strategy offers the most plausible route to co-optimizing the ionic, electronic, and physical structure of the cathode within our fixed platform. A key process risk to monitor will be the potential for mechanofusion to damage the SE's crystallinity, which can reduce ionic conductivity [ID: 314]. Process parameters must be carefully optimized to achieve coating without excessive amorphization.
