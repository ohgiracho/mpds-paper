**To:** Sulfide ASSB Cathode Team
**From:** Research Director
**Date:** September 22, 2024
**Subject:** Decision on Microstructural Strategy for High-Loading NCM811 Cathodes

This memorandum summarizes the recent debate on optimizing our NCM811/LPSCl composite cathode and outlines the path forward. The core challenge is to geometrically allocate our limited LPSCl supply to balance ionic continuity, electronic accessibility, and packing density at >85 wt% active material, all within the fixed constraints of our mechanofusion-based platform.

### 1. Synthesis of Arguments

The debate presented two distinct philosophies for cathode design:

*   **Scientist A (The Architect)** advocated for a pragmatic, process-integrated approach focused on optimizing the geometry of our existing, fixed components. The core proposal is to create a **bimodal LPSCl particle size distribution** within the composite. This strategy allocates fine LPSCl particles to establish ionic contact points on the NCM811 surface, while using coarser LPSCl particles to fill interstitial voids, thereby increasing packing density and forming low-tortuosity "highways" for bulk ion transport (Scientist A [ID: 345, 31]). This approach aims to create the required ionic and electronic networks *in-situ* through a single, controlled dry or minimal-solvent fabrication process (Scientist A [ID: 11]), arguing that this is more scalable and avoids the known chemical incompatibilities of wet-slurry processing with sulfides (Scientist A [ID: 10, 87]).

*   **Scientist B (The Engineer)** argued that the foundational components are chemically unstable and that any geometric arrangement is futile without addressing this first. This "architectural" strategy rejects the project's constraints, proposing a multi-step, bottom-up redesign: (1) pre-coating the NCM811 active material to create a stable artificial CEI (Scientist B [ID: 8, 21]); (2) replacing the unstable LPSCl at the cathode interface with a more stable halide electrolyte (Scientist B [ID: 10, 39]); (3) deactivating the carbon additive via surface coating (Scientist B [ID: 44]); and (4) abandoning mechanofusion in favor of a traditional slurry-casting process with a functional binder to ensure mechanical integrity (Scientist B [ID: 23, 57]). This approach prioritizes interfacial chemical stability above all else.

The fundamental disagreement is whether to optimize the arrangement of the *given* materials (Scientist A) or to fundamentally change the materials before arranging them (Scientist B).

### 2. Director's Assessment and Recommended Path Forward

Scientist B’s points regarding the fundamental chemo-mechanical instabilities at the NCM/sulfide and carbon/sulfide interfaces are valid and represent critical long-term research avenues for next-generation systems. However, the proposed solutions—involving pre-coatings, halide electrolyte substitution, and a complete shift to wet processing—fall explicitly outside the scope and constraints of the current project. Our mandate is to optimize the fixed NCM811/LPSCl/additive system via the specified mechanofusion-based route.

Therefore, **Scientist A’s approach provides the only actionable strategy that directly addresses the problem as defined.**

The core principle of a bimodal LPSCl allocation is the most plausible path to balancing the competing demands of our system. However, Scientist B’s critique of standard mechanofusion—that it can uncontrollably pulverize the SE and degrade its conductivity (Scientist B [ID: 5])—is a significant risk we must mitigate.

**The recommended path forward is as follows:**

We will adopt Scientist A's core strategy of a **bimodal geometric allocation of LPSCl**, dedicating finer particles for surface coverage and coarser particles for interstitial filling. However, we will refine the fabrication process to ensure control. Instead of relying on brute-force mechanofusion to generate the particle sizes, we will explore two parallel paths based on Scientist A’s refined arguments:
1.  **Controlled Dry-Mixing:** Pre-prepare coarse and fine LPSCl fractions and use a low-energy, controlled mechanofusion process designed to promote surface adhesion of the fines without pulverizing the coarse fraction.
2.  **Minimal-Solvent "Liquid-Phase" Fabrication:** Investigate the semi-dry, liquid-phase assisted method (Scientist A [ID: 11]) to use a minimal amount of a compatible solvent to form a dense, uniform composite, achieving the desired bimodal architecture with superior particle-to-particle contact and less mechanical stress than high-pressure dry pressing.

This hybrid approach directly answers the central question of geometric allocation while actively mitigating the key process-related risks identified during the debate.

### 3. Evidence Map for Recommended Composition

This strategy is supported by evidence presented from both sides, which collectively build a strong case for this specific microstructural design.

*   **Core Strategy (Bimodal Allocation):** The principle of using a mixed-particle-size SE to improve overall electrochemical performance is established (Scientist A [ID: 345], Scientist B [ID: 70]). This directly addresses the need to balance local ionic access with bulk transport.
*   **Ionic Continuity:** Fine particles will establish a sufficient density of contact points on the NCM811 surface, mitigating the primary diffusion limitations and high tortuosity that cause polarization (Scientist A [ID: 31, 157]). Coarse particles will form the primary, low-resistance ionic network through the bulk of the thick electrode (Scientist A [ID: 5, 11]).
*   **Electronic Accessibility:** By avoiding a complete, hermetic SE coating, this strategy preserves sufficient NCM surface area for contact with the conductive additive. This is a pragmatic compromise, as minimizing the total reactive carbon/sulfide interface area is a key strategy for enhancing stability (Scientist A [ID: 20]).
*   **Dense Packing & Mechanical Integrity:** The bimodal particle distribution is a classic method for increasing packing density. Furthermore, by pursuing a controlled fabrication method that avoids extreme high-pressure consolidation, we aim to create a dense structure without inducing the microcracks and delamination that lead to chemo-mechanical failure and impedance growth during cycling (Scientist A [ID: 58], Scientist B [ID: 252]). The liquid-phase method, in particular, has been shown to produce low-tortuosity cathodes with high active material content (Scientist A [ID: 11]).

This path forward leverages the most compelling insights from the debate while remaining firmly within our project's scope. Proceed with planning experiments to validate these bimodal architectures immediately.
