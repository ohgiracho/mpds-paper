To effectively host selenium (Se) in MOF-derived bimodally porous carbon nanofibers (CNFs) for Li-Se cathodes, the host architecture must be engineered to balance selenium confinement, ion transport, and electronic percolation. The optimal strategy involves creating a hierarchical structure that physically and chemically immobilizes selenium and its polyselenide intermediates, provides efficient pathways for both Li⁺ ions and electrons, and maintains structural integrity during cycling.

### Design Framework for a High-Performance Li-Se Cathode

A rationally designed cathode using MOF-derived bimodally porous CNFs can overcome the primary failure modes of Li-Se batteries: the dissolution and shuttling of lithium polyselenide (LiPS) intermediates, the poor electronic conductivity of selenium and its discharge products (Li₂Se), and mechanical degradation from the large volume expansion (~80%) of selenium upon lithiation.

1.  **Hierarchical Porosity for Confinement and Transport**: A bimodal pore architecture is essential for separating the functions of active material storage and transport kinetics.
    *   **Intra-fiber Micropores (<2 nm)**: Generated within the carbon nanofibers, these small pores provide strong physical confinement for selenium. This confinement effectively suppresses the dissolution of soluble polyselenide intermediates into the electrolyte, a primary cause of capacity fade Scientist A [ID: 8]. There is evidence that confining selenium in such small pores can alter the electrochemical reaction pathway, potentially favoring a more direct solid-state conversion to Li₂Se and bypassing the formation of highly soluble long-chain polyselenides, which is associated with ultrastable cycling Scientist A [ID: 38], Scientist A [ID: 41].
    *   **Inter-fiber Mesopores (2–50 nm) and Macropores (>50 nm)**: The voids between the individual nanofibers create an interconnected network of larger pores. These are critical for rapid Li⁺ transport by ensuring the electrode is fully wetted with electrolyte Scientist A [ID: 15]. This network also serves as a buffer to accommodate the volume expansion of selenium, preserving the electrode's structural integrity Scientist B [ID: 17]. An architecture lacking sufficient mesoporosity will suffer from transport limitations, leading to poor rate performance and incomplete selenium utilization Scientist B [ID: 26]. This inter-fiber porosity is particularly crucial for achieving high areal capacity, as it creates ion transport highways that prevent ion starvation in thick, high-mass-loading electrodes.

2.  **Chemical Anchoring via Heteroatom Doping**: While physical confinement is necessary, it is often insufficient to fully immobilize polar LiPS species within a carbon host. Introducing nitrogen (N) heteroatoms into the carbon framework creates polar sites that can chemically adsorb LiPS intermediates through strong binding interactions Scientist A [ID: 15], Scientist B [ID: 11]. This chemical anchoring is a key factor in mitigating active material loss and achieving long-term cycling stability Scientist B [ID: 23], Scientist B [ID: 96]. Additionally, N-doping enhances the electronic conductivity of the carbon host, which facilitates faster charge transfer kinetics Scientist A [ID: 15].

3.  **Structural and Electronic Integrity**: The host must form a continuous, three-dimensional (3D) conductive network. An interconnected CNF mat provides robust, long-range pathways for electron transport and mechanical stability, allowing it to be used as a binder-free electrode. This eliminates insulating and electrochemically inactive components, which increases the overall energy density and reduces internal resistance Scientist A [ID: 2], Scientist A [ID: 4].

*   **Precursor Fabrication**: A composite nanofiber precursor is fabricated by electrospinning a solution of PAN containing a homogeneous dispersion of ZIF-8 nanoparticles.
