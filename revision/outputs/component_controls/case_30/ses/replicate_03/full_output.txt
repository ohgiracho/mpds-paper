While the core strategy is agreed upon, the proposals diverge on key processing details. These conflicts are resolved below to form a single, coherent plan.

**Conflict 1: One-Step vs. Two-Step Thermal Treatment**

*   **Scientist A's Position:** Recommends a one-step thermal treatment where the MOF precursor is heated with selenium powder, achieving carbonization and selenization simultaneously (Scientist A [ID: 2], Scientist A [ID: 61]).
*   **Scientist B's Position:** Recommends a two-step process involving initial pyrolysis of the MOF to form a carbon/metal-oxide intermediate, followed by a separate selenization step (Scientist B [ID: 2], Scientist B [ID: 133]).
*   **Resolution:** The one-step process offers greater process simplicity. The two-step process may offer more precise control over the intermediate carbon structure and the final selenide phase composition.
    *   **Recommendation:** Begin with the **one-step thermal treatment** proposed by Scientist A due to its efficiency. If characterization reveals incomplete conversion, undesirable side phases, or a poorly formed carbon matrix, then adopt the **two-step process** from Scientist B. The two-step method should be considered a valuable tool for fine-tuning the material properties if the simpler route proves insufficient for the chosen bimetallic system.
