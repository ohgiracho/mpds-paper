### **A Tiered Protocol for Evaluating α-MnO₂ Cathodes**

This framework is structured in three sequential tiers, moving from isolated component characterization to standardized full-cell benchmarking and finally to in-depth mechanistic analysis. Findings from each tier inform the next; for example, anodes that fail stability criteria in Tier 1 should not proceed to full-cell tests, and key redox potentials identified in Tier 1 should be targeted for mechanistic investigation in Tier 3.

#### **Tier 1: Decoupled Component-Level Characterization**

The objective of this initial tier is to assess the intrinsic properties and stability of the cathode and anode materials independently, minimizing confounding variables.

**1.1. Three-Electrode Cathode Evaluation**
To measure the electrochemical response of the α-MnO₂ cathode while decoupling its potential from the counter electrode, a three-electrode configuration is essential. This provides a more accurate measurement of the cathode's potential and kinetics.

*   **Cell Configuration:** Employ a three-electrode cell with the α-MnO₂ cathode as the working electrode, a stable counter electrode, and a standard reference electrode (e.g., Ag/AgCl).
    *   **Counter Electrode Selection:** An oversized platinum mesh is a suitable counter electrode for kinetic studies where a stable, non-interfering potential is paramount.     *   **Accounting for AC Contribution:** If AC is used, its pseudocapacitive contribution must be quantified and subtracted. This can be achieved by first characterizing the AC electrode in a symmetric (AC||AC) cell using the same electrolyte to determine its specific capacitance and rate behavior. This baseline data is then used to deconvolve the AC's contribution from the three-electrode cell measurements.
*   **Electrolyte Selection and Dissolution Testing:**
    1.  **Intrinsic Dissolution Test:** Use a simple aqueous electrolyte (e.g., 2 M ZnSO₄) to assess the material's inherent tendency to dissolve.
    2.  **Suppressed Dissolution Test:** Separately, use an electrolyte containing Mn²⁺ ions (e.g., 2 M ZnSO₄ + 0.1 M MnSO₄) to evaluate performance when dissolution is suppressed via Le Châtelier's principle Scientist A [ID: 114], Scientist A [ID: 116], Scientist B [ID: 39].     3.  **Quantification:** After cycling to a defined endpoint (e.g., 80% capacity retention, or for 100–500 cycles), analyze the electrolyte for dissolved Mn content using Inductively Coupled Plasma-Optical Emission Spectrometry (ICP-OES) or Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS) Scientist B [ID: 61], Scientist B [ID: 479]. *   **Electrochemical Analysis:**
