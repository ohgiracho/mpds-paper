Within a fixed polycrystalline NCM811/LPSCl/conductive-additive composite prepared by mechanofusion, the geometric allocation of the limited solid electrolyte (SE) is critical for balancing ionic continuity, electronic accessibility, and dense packing at active material (AM) loadings above 85 wt%. The most defensible strategy is to implement a hierarchical microstructure based on a **bimodal particle size distribution for the LPSCl solid electrolyte**. This framework assigns distinct geometric roles to fine and coarse SE particles to systematically manage the fundamental transport and density trade-offs inherent in high-loading composite cathodes.

### Recommended Microstructural Framework

The proposed solution leverages controlled mechanofusion to create a composite cathode architecture with two distinct, functionally specialized populations of LPSCl particles:

1.  **Fine SE Fraction for Partial Surface Coverage:** A sub-fraction of the LPSCl should consist of fine, sub-micron particles. During mechanofusion, these particles are intended to form a partial, non-continuous coating on the surfaces of the larger polycrystalline NCM811 secondary spheres. This configuration establishes a high-surface-area interface for Li-ion injection into the active material, which is critical for alleviating the diffusive limitations that become a primary bottleneck at high AM loadings Scientist A [ID: 31]. Crucially, the coverage must remain partial to preserve sufficient exposed NCM811 surface area for direct contact with the conductive additive (CA). This ensures the integrity of the electronic percolation network, which is essential for charge transfer and high material utilization Scientist B [ID: 20]; Scientist B [ID: 67].

2.  **Coarse SE Fraction for Interstitial Filling:** The remaining, larger fraction of the LPSCl should be composed of coarser, micron-sized particles. These particles are designed to fill the interstitial voids between the large, partially-coated NCM811 secondary spheres. This coarse fraction serves two critical functions:
    *   It forms the primary, long-range ionic conduction backbone, connecting the disparate NCM811 particles and reducing the overall tortuosity of the Li-ion transport path through the thick electrode Scientist A [ID: 157].
    *   It enhances the overall packing density of the composite. A multimodal particle size distribution (large NCM811, coarse LPSCl, fine LPSCl) enables more efficient space-filling, which reduces porosity and increases the volumetric energy density of the final electrode Scientist A [ID: 345]; Scientist B [ID: 70].

This dual-scale geometric arrangement aims to establish two interpenetrating, co-percolating networks—one ionic (LPSCl) and one electronic (CA)—that are both in intimate contact with the NCM811 active material.

### Rationale and Comparison with Alternative Strategies

The proposed bimodal framework is superior to monolithic approaches because it explicitly addresses the competing requirements of a high-loading composite cathode.

*   **Superiority over a Surface-Biased (All-Fine) Strategy:** Using only fine SE particles to maximize surface coverage would risk encapsulating the NCM811 particles in an electronically insulating layer. This would sever connections to the electronic network, isolate active material, and dramatically increase charge transfer resistance, thereby limiting rate capability and utilization Scientist B [ID: 13]; Scientist B [ID: 20]. While maximizing local ionic contact, this approach creates a fatal electronic bottleneck.

*   **Superiority over an Interstitial-Biased (All-Coarse) Strategy:** Using only coarse SE particles as interstitial filler would maximize NCM811-CA contact, ensuring good electronic conductivity. However, it would create large, ionically disconnected surface areas on the NCM811 particles, restricting ionic transport to point-like contacts. This configuration leads to high interfacial resistance, severe Li-ion diffusion limitations, and significant underutilization of the active material, particularly at practical current densities Scientist A [ID: 5]; Scientist A [ID: 31].

The bimodal approach mitigates these extremes. The fine LPSCl fraction ensures local ionic pathways are available across the NCM811 surface, while the preserved bare patches allow for robust electronic contact. The coarse LPSCl fraction then integrates these locally-connected particles into a low-tortuosity, long-range ionic network Scientist A [ID: 157]. This hierarchical design principle is validated by studies confirming that composite cathodes fabricated with mixed-particle-size SEs outperform those with uniform-sized SEs Scientist A [ID: 345]; Scientist B [ID: 70]. Furthermore, the high packing density achieved is critical for maintaining mechanical integrity under the high stack pressures required for sulfide-based all-solid-state batteries, which helps minimize void formation and contact loss during cycling Scientist B [ID: 4]; Scientist B [ID: 48].

### Mechanofusion Process Optimization

Mechanofusion, a dry-coating technology, provides the mechanical energy necessary to promote the adhesion of fine particles onto larger core particles while mixing the bulk components Scientist A [ID: 316]; Scientist B [ID: 411]. The process parameters, such as energy input and duration, are critical control levers. Optimization should aim to find a process window with sufficient energy to adhere the fine LPSCl particles to the NCM811 surface, but not so much energy that the soft SE deforms excessively and creates a complete, encapsulating layer Scientist B [ID: 5]; Scientist B [ID: 314]. It is imperative that the mechanical stress is managed to preserve the structural integrity of the polycrystalline NCM811 secondary spheres, as excessive energy can induce microcracks that accelerate chemo-mechanical degradation and shorten cycle life Scientist A [ID: 58]; Scientist B [ID: 76].

### Inference, Uncertainty, and Strategic Optimization

**Inference:** It is inferred that by assigning distinct geometric roles to different SE particle populations—local ionic access via fine particles and long-range ionic transport plus dense packing via coarse particles—the bimodal allocation strategy provides the most robust and defensible framework for resolving the ionic-electronic-density trilemma in high-loading cathodes.

**Uncertainty and Strategic Optimization:** The successful implementation of this framework is contingent on navigating several interdependent variables. The following factors represent the primary axes for systematic experimental optimization.

*   **Quantitative Allocation of Non-Active Mass:** The <15 wt% non-active mass must be strategically partitioned between the fine SE, coarse SE, and the CA. The CA loading must first be set above the minimum threshold required for electronic percolation. The remaining mass should be allocated to the SE, with the fine-to-coarse ratio guided by the NCM811 surface area to be partially covered and the interstitial void volume to be filled.
*   **Fine SE Particle Size Optimization:** A critical trade-off exists for the fine SE fraction. The particles must be small enough to adhere to the NCM811 surface and provide a high contact area, but excessive mechanical milling to produce very fine particles can reduce the intrinsic ionic conductivity of the LPSCl by introducing disorder Scientist B [ID: 5]; Scientist B [ID: 314]. The optimal size balances the kinetic benefit of surface area against this degradation in bulk transport property.
*   **Coarse SE Particle Size Optimization:** The size of the coarse SE particles should be optimized to be large enough for efficient interstitial packing but small enough relative to the NCM811 spheres to ensure a sufficient number of inter-particle contact points, thereby maintaining robust connectivity between the NCM surface and the bulk ionic network.
*   **Process Control for Target Microstructure:** A significant uncertainty lies in the practical ability of mechanofusion to reliably produce the desired partial-coverage morphology. Achieving sufficient adhesion without causing complete encapsulation requires careful and systematic optimization of the process parameters.
*   **Inherent Material Stability:** This geometric strategy manages transport networks but does not eliminate underlying material challenges. The composite must still withstand chemo-mechanical stresses from NCM811 volume changes during cycling, which can lead to void formation and contact loss Scientist B [ID: 48]; Scientist B [ID: 180]; Scientist B [ID: 270]. Furthermore, (electro)chemical reactivity at the interface between carbon-based additives and the LPSCl electrolyte remains a risk for long-term stability Scientist A [ID: 20]; Scientist B [ID: 7]; Scientist B [ID: 14].
