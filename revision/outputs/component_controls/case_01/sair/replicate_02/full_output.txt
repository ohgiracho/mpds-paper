### **Optimal Geometric Allocation of Solid Electrolyte in a High-Loading NCM811/LPSCl Composite Cathode**

Within a fixed polycrystalline NCM811/LPSCl/conductive-additive composite prepared by mechanofusion, the geometric allocation of a limited volume of solid electrolyte (SE) is a critical design parameter for balancing ionic continuity, electronic accessibility, and dense packing at active material loadings greater than 85 wt%. The most defensible strategy is a hierarchical microstructural framework based on a **bimodal particle size distribution (PSD) of the LPSCl solid electrolyte**. This approach partitions the SE into two functionally distinct populations:

1.  **Fine-Particle Fraction for Partial Surface Coverage:** A population of fine, sub-micron LPSCl particles designed to adhere to the surface of the larger, micron-scale polycrystalline NCM811 secondary spheres. This fraction establishes a high-area ionic interface on the active material (CAM).
2.  **Coarse-Particle Fraction for Interstitial Filling:** A population of coarser, micron-scale LPSCl particles sized to efficiently fill the interstitial voids between the packed NCM811 spheres. This fraction creates a low-tortuosity bulk ionic backbone.

This bimodal framework is proposed as the optimal strategy because it most effectively addresses the conflicting transport and density requirements inherent to high-loading composite cathodes.

#### **Scientific Justification**

The performance of a high-loading composite cathode is dictated by the efficiency of the interpenetrating networks for ion and electron transport. At >85 wt% CAM, Li-ion diffusion becomes a primary rate-limiting step, as long transport pathways and insufficient CAM-SE contact create "ionically dead" zones that are inaccessible for electrochemical reaction (Scientist A [ID: 31], Scientist B [ID: 12]). Simultaneously, the insulating SE must be arranged to avoid disrupting the electronic percolation network formed by the conductive additive (CA) (Scientist B [ID: 13], Scientist B [ID: 67]). The bimodal SE architecture addresses these challenges at distinct length scales.

**1. Optimizing Ionic Transport and Interfacial Contact**

The bimodal PSD creates a more effective ionic network than a monomodal distribution.

*   **At the Particle Surface:** The fine LPSCl fraction provides a high-fidelity, conformal ionic interface on the NCM811 surface, minimizing charge-transfer resistance and ensuring uniform Li-ion access (Scientist A [ID: 31]). Using only coarse SE particles would result in poor, point-like contacts, leading to high interfacial resistance and large ionically inaccessible surface areas (Scientist B [ID: 12], Scientist B [ID: 270]).
*   **In the Bulk Electrode:** The coarse LPSCl fraction efficiently fills the large interstitial voids between NCM811 particles. This forms an interconnected, low-tortuosity pathway for long-range Li-ion transport through the thick electrode structure, which is critical for maintaining rate capability (Scientist A [ID: 157]). Composite cathodes fabricated with mixed-size SEs have demonstrated superior performance, an effect attributed to the creation of a more efficient and densely packed ionic network (Scientist A [ID: 345], Scientist B [ID: 70]).

**2. Balancing Electronic Accessibility and Mitigating Side Reactions**

While establishing ionic contact, the SE geometry must not compromise the electronic network or exacerbate chemical instability. This introduces a critical trade-off at the NCM811 surface.

*   **Competition for Surface Access:** The fine SE fraction, while essential for ionic contact, directly competes with the CA for access to the NCM811 surface. **Inference:** An excessively thick or complete SE coating could create "electronically dead" zones by physically blocking the CA, thereby increasing electronic resistance at the charge-transfer interface. The optimal geometry therefore involves only partial surface coverage by the fine SE, creating a well-distributed triple-phase boundary where CAM, SE, and CA meet.
*   **Mitigating Side Reactions:** Sulfide SEs are known to react with carbon-based CAs, forming resistive interfacial layers that degrade performance (Scientist B [ID: 7], Scientist B [ID: 20], Scientist B [ID: 44]). The bimodal approach presents a complex trade-off in this regard. In the bulk interstitial spaces, using coarse SE particles minimizes the total SE-CA contact area compared to filling the same volume with fine particles, which should theoretically slow degradation. However, the high-surface-area fine SE fraction at the CAM interface will be in intimate contact with the CA network. **Uncertainty:** It is uncertain whether the reduced reaction rate in the bulk outweighs the potentially accelerated degradation at the critical CAM-SE-CA interface. The success of the electronic network also depends on the CA's ability to form a robust percolating web around the coarser SE obstacles, a task for which high-aspect-ratio CAs are better suited (Scientist B [ID: 20], Scientist B [ID: 67]).

**3. Maximizing Packing Density and Volumetric Energy Density**

High volumetric energy density requires minimizing non-active void volume. A bimodal or multimodal PSD is a well-established materials science principle for achieving higher packing density than a monomodal distribution (Scientist B [ID: 70]). In this framework, the large NCM811 spheres form the primary packed bed. The coarse LPSCl particles then efficiently fill the large interstitial voids between them, and the fine LPSCl particles subsequently fill the smaller remaining voids. This hierarchical packing strategy minimizes porosity, leading directly to a higher composite density and, consequently, a higher volumetric energy density (Scientist B [ID: 48], Scientist B [ID: 119]).

#### **Implementation via Mechanofusion**

The proposed bimodal architecture is hypothesized to be achievable in a single-step mechanofusion process by leveraging the distinct mechanical properties of the components. Sulfide SEs are mechanically soft and deformable (Scientist B [ID: 309]), whereas NCM811 secondary particles are comparatively hard and brittle.

*   **Hypothesized Mechanism:** During co-milling of the NCM811, CA, and bimodal LPSCl powders, the high-energy mechanical forces may cause the fine, soft LPSCl particles to preferentially deform and adhere to the surfaces of the larger NCM811 spheres, forming the desired partial coating. Concurrently, the coarser, more mechanically robust LPSCl particles are expected to resist significant deformation and primarily occupy the interstitial spaces. Mechanofusion is a process known to be capable of producing such composite and core-shell structures (Scientist A [ID: 316], Scientist B [ID: 411]).
*   **Process Control and Risks:** This proposed self-assembly is a hypothesis contingent on precise process control. Excessive mechanical energy can fracture the polycrystalline NCM811 particles, compromising their structural integrity (Scientist A [ID: 58]), or amorphize the LPSCl, reducing its ionic conductivity (Scientist B [ID: 5], Scientist B [ID: 314]). Insufficient energy will result in poor mixing and inadequate adhesion, failing to form the percolating networks. The optimal mechanofusion parameters (e.g., rotation speed, duration) must be empirically determined.

#### **Inferences, Uncertainties, and Key Trade-offs**

*   **Inference:** A bimodal LPSCl particle distribution provides the most robust geometric framework for balancing the conflicting requirements of ionic access, electronic percolation, and high packing density in a high-loading composite cathode. Its success is contingent on achieving a partial, not complete, surface coating to preserve electronic pathways.

*   **Uncertainty:** The optimal ratio of fine-to-coarse LPSCl, their precise particle sizes, and the ideal degree of CAM surface coverage are not known *a priori* and represent key parameters for empirical optimization. The degree to which the hypothesized self-assembly occurs during single-step mechanofusion is a primary process uncertainty.

*   **Key Trade-offs:**
    *   **Ionic vs. Electronic Access at the CAM Surface:** This is the central trade-off. Increasing SE surface coverage improves ionic contact but risks isolating the CAM from the electronic network. The optimal structure must balance these competing requirements at the triple-phase boundary.
    *   **Initial Performance vs. Mechanical Durability:** The recommended high-density, rigid composite structure is beneficial for initial electrochemical performance. However, this rigidity can poorly accommodate the anisotropic volume changes of polycrystalline NCM811 during cycling, potentially concentrating stress and accelerating mechanical failure via particle cracking and contact loss (Scientist A [ID: 213], Scientist B [ID: 4], Scientist B [ID: 76]).
    *   **Chemical Stability:** The proposed geometric solution addresses transport and packing but does not eliminate fundamental chemo-mechanical degradation. The intrinsic reactivity between Ni-rich NCM811 and sulfide SEs will still form a resistive interphase over time (Scientist B [ID: 14], Scientist B [ID: 31], Scientist B [ID: 381]). The bimodal approach may reduce SE-CA reactions in the bulk but could localize them at the critical CAM interface.
