### 1. Diagnosis: The Voids Are Not Enough

Your core problem is that you are designing for passive volume accommodation. A hollow sphere that simply expands and contracts will eventually fail. The primary particles will lose electrical contact with each other and the current collector, leading to rapid capacity fade, especially at high rates. Your focus should shift from creating *empty* voids to creating a *functional, hierarchical architecture*. ### 2. Proposed Solution: Engineer a Carbon-Integrated, Hierarchical Microsphere

Your spray-drying precursor should include a polymer that, upon thermal treatment, pyrolyzes into a conductive carbon matrix that coats the primary `NiMoO4` particles and acts as a binder within the microsphere. A polymer-assisted solution method has been shown to be effective for creating carbon-coated `MoO2` on nickel foam, where the carbon serves as an effective binder and improves intimate contact between the active material and the current collector [ID: 116]. 1.  **Electronic Wiring:** It maintains electrical continuity between the `NiMoO4` primary particles even as they undergo volume changes, which is essential for high-rate capability and preventing "dead" material.

The use of carbonaceous materials like reduced graphene oxide (rGO) in hollow `MoS2` microspheres has been shown to promote charge transport and facilitate electrolyte diffusion, leading to high specific capacitance and good stability [ID: 49].
