Based on the evidence provided, a testing protocol and evaluation framework to reliably distinguish intrinsic α-MnO₂ behavior from various artifacts in aqueous zinc-ion batteries (ZIBs) would involve a multi-pronged approach combining controlled cell configurations, systematic electrochemical testing, advanced in-situ and ex-situ characterization, and standardized reporting metrics.

### 1. Cell Configuration and Component Selection to Minimize Extrinsic Artifacts

A primary challenge is the interference from the zinc anode and other cell components. The protocol should first aim to isolate the cathode's performance by minimizing these variables.

*   **Three-Electrode System:** To decouple the cathode's electrochemical signature from the Zn anode's potential drift, corrosion, and dendrite formation, a three-electrode cell is the most rigorous configuration. This allows for direct measurement of the α-MnO₂ working electrode potential against a stable reference electrode. While the provided evidence does not specify a standard reference electrode for these electrolytes, this setup is a fundamental electrochemical practice for isolating electrode behavior.

*   **Stabilized Two-Electrode System:** If a two-electrode setup is used, it is critical to employ a highly stable and reversible Zn anode to minimize its contribution to cell failure. The evidence describes several strategies for this, including:
    *   **Surface Coatings:** Applying protective layers like N-doped graphene oxide [ID: 510, 638], ZnF₂ [ID: 513, 531], or covalent organic frameworks [ID: 682] can suppress dendrite growth and side reactions.
    *   **3D Hosts:** Using 3D conductive hosts like carbon nanofiber frameworks can guide uniform Zn deposition and suppress dendrites [ID: 792, 850].
    *   **Electrolyte Additives:** Incorporating additives such as ammonium cations [ID: 41], β-cyclodextrin [ID: 527], or sericin [ID: 548] can modify the Zn solvation shell and promote dendrite-free plating.
    *   **Anode Characterization:** The stability of the chosen Zn anode configuration should be independently verified through long-term cycling in a symmetric Zn||Zn cell, reporting polarization voltage and cycle life [ID: 41, 513, 541].

*   **Binder and Separator Selection:** The choice of binder and separator can influence performance. Water-based binders like sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) have been shown to improve adhesion, wettability, and electrochemical performance compared to PVdF [ID: 3, 346]. The separator type (e.g., glass fiber vs. polymer) should be standardized and reported, as it can affect ion transport and dendrite penetration [ID: 135].

### 2. Electrolyte Formulation and pH Control

Electrolyte composition is a major source of artifacts, particularly pH-driven side reactions and Mn dissolution.

*   **Baseline and Buffered Electrolytes:** A baseline test should be conducted in a simple, widely used electrolyte such as 2 M ZnSO₄ [ID: 538, 794]. To specifically address Mn dissolution and associated capacity fade, a parallel test should use an electrolyte containing MnSO₄ (e.g., 2 M ZnSO₄ + 0.1 M MnSO₄) [ID: 116, 142, 538]. The added Mn²⁺ creates a common-ion effect that can suppress the dissolution of Mn from the cathode [ID: 116, 237, 399].

*   **pH Monitoring and Control:** The reaction mechanism is highly sensitive to pH changes. Lee et al. [ID: 27] demonstrated that Mn dissolution during discharge increases local pH, leading to the precipitation of insulating zinc hydroxide sulfate (ZHS, Zn₄(OH)₆(SO₄)·5H₂O) on the cathode surface, which is a major artifact. A robust protocol must include:
    *   *In operando* pH measurement near the cathode surface using pH indicators or a pH microelectrode to quantify local pH evolution during cycling [ID: 538].
    *   Testing in electrolytes designed to buffer pH or manage proton activity, such as those with formate anions [ID: 553] or through structured hydrogel electrolytes that can create localized pH environments [ID: 89].

*   **Decoupling H⁺ and Zn²⁺ Roles:** The charge storage mechanism in MnO₂ is a subject of debate, with evidence supporting H⁺ insertion, Zn²⁺ insertion, or a co-insertion process [ID: 6, 14, 26, 65, 103, 225]. To distinguish these contributions, testing should be performed in electrolytes that modulate water activity and proton availability. This includes:
    *   Nonaqueous electrolytes like dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), which can suppress hydrogen evolution and alter the intercalation species [ID: 16, 61, 84].
    *   "Water-in-salt" electrolytes (WiSE), which have limited "free" water, thereby suppressing H⁺-related reactions and promoting Zn²⁺-dominant intercalation [ID: 345, 374].

### 3. Standardized Electrochemical Evaluation Protocol

A consistent testing sequence is necessary to generate comparable data and distinguish different electrochemical processes.

1.  **Cyclic Voltammetry (CV):** Perform CV scans at various rates (e.g., 0.1 to 10 mV/s) to identify redox peaks corresponding to different reactions (e.g., H⁺/Zn²⁺ intercalation, conversion reactions) and to analyze the proportion of diffusion-controlled versus pseudocapacitive charge storage [ID: 8, 28, 67, 142].
2.  **Galvanostatic Cycling with Potential Limitation (GCPL):** Conduct cycling tests at a moderate rate (e.g., 0.2C–1C, based on a theoretical capacity of 308 mAh g⁻¹ for a one-electron process). Crucially, the discharge voltage should be limited to above ~1.2 V vs. Zn/Zn²⁺ to avoid the highly irreversible conversion reactions that occur at lower potentials, which are a primary cause of capacity fade [ID: 286]. Long-term cycling stability tests (e.g., >1000 cycles) should be performed at a higher rate (e.g., ≥2C) [ID: 51, 60].
3.  **Rate Capability Testing:** Evaluate the cathode at a wide range of current densities (e.g., 0.1A g⁻¹ to 10A g⁻¹) to probe kinetic limitations related to ion transport and electronic conductivity [ID: 8, 28, 58].
4.  **Kinetic Analysis:** Employ the Galvanostatic Intermittent Titration Technique (GITT) to measure the chemical diffusion coefficients of the charge carriers (H⁺/Zn²⁺) within the α-MnO₂ structure [ID: 8, 14, 20, 78]. Use Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) at various states of charge to monitor changes in charge-transfer resistance (Rct) and solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation [ID: 8, 410].

### 4. Mechanistic Characterization Framework

To link electrochemical signatures to physical and chemical changes, a combination of ex-situ and in-situ characterization is required.

*   **Structural and Phase Evolution:** Use *ex-situ* and, ideally, *in-situ* X-ray diffraction (XRD) to track changes in the α-MnO₂ crystal structure, including lattice parameter shifts, phase transformations (e.g., to spinel or layered structures), and the formation of crystalline byproducts like ZHS [ID: 11, 20, 27, 99, 105].
*   **Surface Chemistry and Valence State:** Employ *ex-situ* X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) on electrodes stopped at different charge/discharge states to analyze the Mn oxidation state (e.g., Mn⁴⁺/Mn³⁺/Mn²⁺ ratios), confirm the deposition of ZHS, and detect other surface species [ID: 20, 55, 117].
*   **Local Structure and Byproduct Formation:** Utilize *in-situ* Raman spectroscopy to provide real-time information on the vibrational modes of the Mn-O lattice, which are sensitive to local structure, protonation, and the formation of species like ZHS [ID: 30, 78, 84].
*   **Morphology and Dissolution:** After cycling, analyze the cathode morphology with Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) to observe particle cracking, pulverization, or surface deposits [ID: 11, 404]. Quantify Mn dissolution by analyzing the electrolyte composition using Inductively Coupled Plasma-Optical Emission Spectroscopy (ICP-OES) [ID: 264, 399].

### 5. Standardized Reporting for Cross-Study Comparison

To address the unreliability of cross-study comparisons, the evaluation framework must enforce strict reporting standards.

*   **Electrode Parameters:** Report active material mass loading (in mg cm⁻²), electrode composition (active material:conductive carbon:binder ratio), binder type [ID: 3, 346], and electrode thickness. It is advisable to test at both low (<2 mg cm⁻²) and high (>5 mg cm⁻²) mass loadings to reveal transport limitations [ID: 9].
*   **Cell Parameters:** Clearly state the anode material and its excess ratio relative to the cathode (N/P ratio), the electrolyte composition and volume, and the separator type and thickness [ID: 407, 850].
*   **Performance Metrics:** Report capacity normalized to the active material mass (mAh g⁻¹), areal capacity (mAh cm⁻²), current density (A g⁻¹ and mA cm⁻²), and the basis for C-rate calculation (e.g., theoretical capacity for one- or two-electron transfer) [ID: 88, 221]. When possible, full-cell energy density (Wh kg⁻¹) should be reported based on the total mass of the electrodes [ID: 113].

By systematically implementing this framework, researchers can more effectively decouple the intrinsic electrochemical behavior of α-MnO₂ from the numerous artifacts that arise from the complex interplay between the cathode, electrolyte, and anode in aqueous ZIBs. The evidence strongly suggests that phenomena like pH shifts, Mn dissolution, and ZHS formation are not intrinsic to MnO₂ but are artifacts of the overall cell system, which can be controlled and understood through this rigorous protocol.
