Based on my data, the challenges you describe with metal sulfide anodes in potassium-ion batteries (KIBs) are well-documented and stem from fundamental material properties and reaction mechanisms.

**Problem Diagnosis**

1.  **Severe Volume Expansion and Sluggish Kinetics:** The large ionic radius of K+ exacerbates the inherent problems of metal sulfide anodes, leading to severe volume expansion, structural collapse, and sluggish reaction kinetics compared to Li-ion or Na-ion systems [ID: 126, 184]. This structural instability is a primary cause of rapid capacity decay.

2.  **Polysulfide Dissolution and Irreversible Side Reactions:** A critical failure mechanism, particularly in the ether-based electrolytes often used for KIBs, is the dissolution of unstable intermediate potassium polysulfides (KxSy) [ID: 49, 348]. This process leads to the loss of active material and poor reaction reversibility, resulting in an inferior cycle life [ID: 49].

3.  **Ineffective Void Space Utilization:** While hollow structures are designed to buffer volume changes, a single, large void in a simple yolk-shell design can be structurally inefficient. The core can detach from the shell, leading to a loss of electrical contact and rendering the void space useless after initial cycles.

**Proposed Solution**

To address these interconnected issues, I propose a composite strategy integrating a **multi-yolk-shell (m-Y-S) architecture** with **heterostructure engineering** and a **template-assisted synthesis process**.

1.  **Structural Design: Multi-Yolk-Shell (m-Y-S) Architecture**
    Instead of a single core within a hollow shell, an m-Y-S design should be adopted. In this architecture, multiple smaller metal sulfide nanoparticles (the "yolks") are confined within a larger hollow carbon shell [ID: 57]. This design offers several advantages:
    *   **Enhanced Stability:** The distributed void space around multiple yolks is more effective at accommodating the large volume variation during potassiation without causing catastrophic failure of the entire particle [ID: 57]. This approach has been shown to enable ultra-stable potassium storage for thousands of cycles in Fe1-xS@C systems [ID: 57].
    *   **Robust Electron Transport:** The multiple connection points between the yolks and the outer carbon shell create a redundant and stable conductive network, ensuring good electronic contact is maintained throughout extended cycling [ID: 57].

2.  **Interfacial and Chemical Stabilization: Heterostructure Engineering**
    To combat the dissolution of KxSy intermediates, the metal sulfide yolks should be engineered into a heterostructure with a polar material.
    *   **Polysulfide Anchoring:** Introducing a polar metal sulfide, such as VS4, can create anchoring sites that chemically trap the KxSy intermediates via strong adsorption [ID: 49]. This "entrapment effect" stabilizes the intermediates, enhances conversion reversibility, and significantly improves cycle life [ID: 49]. Theoretical calculations confirm that such heterostructures can regulate the distribution of KxSy and maintain the structural integrity of the primary active material [ID: 49].
    *   **Improved Kinetics:** The built-in electric fields at the heterostructure interface can also lower the diffusion energy barrier for K+ ions, promoting faster reaction kinetics and improving rate performance [ID: 125, 39].

3.  **Synthesis Process: Template-Assisted Spray Pyrolysis**
    To address the process concern of creating a consistent hierarchical structure via spray drying, a washable template method is recommended.
    *   **Controlled Porosity:** A facile and scalable method involves using NaCl as a washable template during spray pyrolysis [ID: 29]. The NaCl template helps form well-defined "nanorooms" or voids within the carbon-metal sulfide microspheres. After synthesis, the NaCl can be easily washed away, leaving a hierarchical and porous structure without the risk of collapse that can occur in template-free methods [ID: 29]. This approach has been demonstrated to be a general method for various metal sulfides and is suitable for pilot-scale production [ID: 22, 29].

In summary, a multi-yolk-shell architecture will provide the necessary mechanical stability to withstand the large volume changes inherent to potassiation [ID: 57]. Integrating a polar heterostructure component will chemically stabilize the system by trapping polysulfide intermediates, preventing active material loss [ID: 49]. Finally, employing a washable NaCl template during spray pyrolysis will ensure the reliable, scalable fabrication of the desired hierarchical, porous microspheres [ID: 29]. This combined approach directly addresses the structural, transport, and processing bottlenecks you have identified.
