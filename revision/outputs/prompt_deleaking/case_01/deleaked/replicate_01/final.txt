**To:** Sulfide ASSB Cathode Team
**From:** Research Director
**Date:** October 26, 2024
**Subject:** Decision on High-Loading NCM811 Cathode Strategy

This memorandum outlines the final decision regarding our process strategy for the high-loading NCM811/LPSCl cathode project. Following a thorough review of the competing proposals from Scientist A (The Architect) and Scientist B (The Engineer), a hybrid path forward has been selected.

### 1. Synthesis of Arguments

The debate centered on two fundamentally different philosophies for constructing a high-loading (>85 wt% AM) cathode compatible with mechanofusion.

*   **Scientist A (The Architect)** advocated for a solvent-free, dry-processing route focused on creating an engineered, hierarchical microstructure. The final proposal involved mixing NCM811, carbon additives, and a passivation agent, followed by mechanofusion for densification and a rapid thermal anneal (RTA). This would theoretically create a dense, all-inorganic structure with a protective interface formed *in-situ*. The primary advantages cited were the elimination of performance-limiting binders and reactive solvents (Scientist A [ID: 10, 87]), leading to higher theoretical volumetric energy density (Scientist A [ID: 29]).

*   **Scientist B (The Engineer)** argued for an "interface-first" strategy that prioritizes chemical stability using proven, scalable manufacturing techniques. The final proposal involved first applying a stable, *ex-situ* coating (e.g., a halide like Li₃InCl₆) to the NCM811 particles (Scientist B [ID: 10]). This stabilized material would then be processed via slurry-casting with an ion-conducting binder to create a mechanically compliant and chemically stable electrode (Scientist B [ID: 4, 18]). The core advantages cited were the direct mitigation of the primary chemical failure mode (Scientist B [ID: 14, 306]) and the use of manufacturable, low-risk processes.

### 2. Director's Assessment and Recommended Path Forward

Both scientists presented compelling arguments, but each proposal carries significant risk. Scientist A's *in-situ* passivation via RTA is an elegant concept but represents an uncontrolled, high-risk chemical experiment with a high probability of creating detrimental byproducts rather than a clean interface. Scientist B's reliance on slurry-casting, while manufacturable, deviates from the project's mechanofusion constraint and introduces solvents and binders that we ultimately aim to eliminate.

Therefore, we will proceed with a hybrid strategy, designated **"Interfacially-Stabilized Dry Mechanofusion."** This approach synthesizes the low-risk chemical principles of Scientist B with the solvent-free processing framework of Scientist A.

The recommended path is as follows:

1.  **Adopt Scientist B's foundational principle of pre-passivation.** The primary failure mode in this system is the chemical instability at the NCM/LPSCl interface (Scientist B [ID: 14, 41, 306]). We will mitigate this risk upfront by implementing a verified *ex-situ* coating on the polycrystalline NCM811 particles before they are introduced into the main process. This provides a controlled, stable surface.

2.  **Adopt Scientist A's solvent-free processing framework.** We will proceed with a dry-mixing process compatible with mechanofusion. This aligns with the project constraints and the long-term goal of developing a truly solid-state, high-density manufacturing route (Scientist A [ID: 29]).

3.  **Execute the integrated process.** A dry mixture of the **pre-coated NCM811**, LPSCl, and a synergistic carbon additive network will be densified using a **controlled, low-energy mechanofusion** process. The goal is to achieve intimate particle-particle contact and high packing density (Scientist A [ID: 252]) without the destructive energy or high-temperature reactions proposed in Scientist A's final plan.

This hybrid strategy directly addresses the most critical chemical failure mode while adhering to the project's core processing constraints. It avoids the uncontrolled chemistry of the *in-situ* approach and the performance/process compromises of the slurry-based method.

### 3. Evidence Map for Recommended Composition

The initial composition and process for validation will be based on the following evidence-backed choices from the debate:

| Component/Process | Specification | Rationale & Supporting Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Active Material** | Polycrystalline NCM811 | Fixed project component. |
| **Surface Passivation** | *Ex-situ* coating (e.g., Li₃InCl₆ or LiNbO₃) | Provides a stable, controlled barrier to prevent direct NCM/LPSCl reaction, which is a primary degradation mechanism. **(Scientist B [ID: 10, 21, 306])** |
| **Solid Electrolyte** | LPSCl | Fixed project component. Particle size will be a key variable to optimize for packing and conductivity. **(Scientist B [ID: 5])** |
| **Conductive Additive** | Hybrid CNT/CNF network | A bimodal carbon network is proposed to ensure robust electronic percolation at both local and long-range scales within a dry-mixed composite. **(Scientist A [ID: 20, 325])** |
| **Fabrication Process** | Controlled, low-energy mechanofusion | To achieve a dense, binder-free composite by refining electrolyte particle size and ensuring intimate solid-solid contact without gross mechanical damage. **(Scientist A [ID: 314], Scientist B [ID: 35])** |
| **Target Microstructure** | Dense, binder-free composite with pre-passivated active material | Maximizes volumetric energy density and eliminates solvent/binder-related side reactions and impedance sources. **(Scientist A [ID: 29], Scientist A [ID: 252])** |
