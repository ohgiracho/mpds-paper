    The challenges you have identified—large volume changes, low conductivity, and structural collapse—are characteristic of metal chalcogenide anodes, particularly for sodium-ion batteries (SIBs) where the larger Na-ion exacerbates these issues [ID: 11, 14].     *   **Structural Buffering:** The void spaces between the constituent nanosheets provide distributed buffering for volume expansion throughout the microsphere, mitigating strain more effectively than a single central void [ID: 11]. This prevents the catastrophic pulverization of the entire structure.
    *   **Ion Accessibility:** This open, porous architecture creates a high electrode/electrolyte contact area and provides numerous pathways for rapid Na-ion diffusion into the interior of the microsphere, which is superior to diffusion through a thick, solid shell [ID: 11].

    *   **Multicomponent Chalcogenide:** The use of a double transition-metal system (Mo/Ni) is a sound strategy.     *   **Electronic Continuity:** To overcome the inherently low conductivity of the chalcogenide phase, the hierarchical microspheres should be homogeneously integrated with reduced graphene oxide (RGO) nanosheets [ID: 11, 69]. The RGO acts as a highly conductive framework that ensures electronic continuity throughout the entire microsphere, prevents the aggregation of the chalcogenide nanosheets, and provides mechanical reinforcement to the structure [ID: 13, 69].

4.  **Process Scalability:**
    Your concern regarding process scalability can be addressed. Such hierarchical RGO-chalcogenide composites can be fabricated using facile, low-cost, and scalable methods.
    *   **One-Pot Solvothermal/Hydrothermal Synthesis:** This approach allows for the simultaneous reduction of graphene oxide and the in-situ growth and assembly of the metal chalcogenide nanosheets onto the graphene framework [ID: 11, 69].     *   **Microwave-Assisted Synthesis:** For rapid production, a microwave-assisted technique could be employed. In summary, a hierarchical microsphere composed of interconnected Mo/Ni-chalcogenide nanosheets anchored on an RGO scaffold directly addresses the main bottlenecks. This design provides distributed void space for strain, high surface area for ion access, and a continuous conductive network, all within a structure that maintains good packing density and can be produced via scalable solution-based methods [ID: 11, 69].
