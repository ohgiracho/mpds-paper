[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background:
 We are developing metal sulfoselenide solid-solution nanoparticles embedded in hollow carbon nanospheres for potassium-ion storage, where the architecture has to handle even larger ion-induced strain than typical Li or Na systems.
- Main bottleneck:
 Potassium storage amplifies volume change and contact loss, so bare conversion materials degrade quickly unless the structure provides real stress accommodation.
- Transport bottleneck:
 Metal sulfoselenide solid-solution nanoparticles embedded in hollow carbon nanospheres should maintain electron transport and electrolyte access, yet dense shells or excessive inactive scaffolds can still choke K-ion transport through the secondary particle.
- Structural bottleneck:
 The internal buffering space must remain mechanically useful during repeated cycling; otherwise the particle turns into inactive carbon plus detached active fragments.
- Process concern:
 Hetero-anion engineering has to assemble the hierarchical particle without collapsing the void network or introducing broad particle-to-particle variability.
