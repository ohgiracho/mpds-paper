[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background:
 We are designing trimodally porous N-doped carbon frameworks as selenium immobilizers as a selenium host for Li-Se cathodes, where confinement and conductivity must be solved together.
- Main bottleneck:
 Selenium needs a host that limits active-species loss and interfacial degradation without sacrificing usable loading or long-cycle reversibility.
- Transport bottleneck:
 Trimodally porous N-doped carbon frameworks as selenium immobilizers should improve electron transport and electrolyte access, yet an overpacked host or the wrong pore hierarchy can trap transport instead of accelerating it.
- Structural bottleneck:
 The carbon host must remain continuous after selenium loading and cycling; otherwise the framework separates into poorly wired domains with uneven selenium utilization.
- Process concern:
 Pore-structure engineering has to create the intended pore hierarchy and selenium-host interface reproducibly rather than producing random porosity or poorly filled regions.
