[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background:
 We are evaluating alpha-MnO2 cathodes in aqueous zinc-ion cells, and the main objective is to isolate intrinsic cathode behavior from protocol-dependent artifacts.
- Main bottleneck:
 Apparent ZIB performance can be distorted by Mn dissolution, parasitic reactions, pH-driven side products, and unstable Zn counter-electrode behavior rather than the alpha-MnO2 cathode itself.
- Transport bottleneck:
 Electrochemical signatures can change with amorphous-versus-crystalline alpha-MnO2, electrolyte composition, and cell configuration, so transport-related conclusions are easy to misread when the protocol is inconsistent.
- Structural bottleneck:
 Mass loading, current density, separator choice, electrolyte volume, and Zn anode condition all change the apparent cycling stability, making cross-study comparison unreliable.
- Process concern:
 Electrochemical testing protocol has to define a rigorous evaluation workflow rather than introduce another material modification that still cannot be interpreted consistently.
