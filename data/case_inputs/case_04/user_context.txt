[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background:
 We are designing V2O3 anchored on CNT microspheres with macrovoids as an aqueous zinc-ion battery cathode, where ion access, electronic continuity, and aqueous structural stability must all be balanced.
- Main bottleneck:
 Aqueous ZIB cathodes need fast Zn2+ transport, but interfacial instability and repeated structural stress still degrade reversibility if the architecture is poorly chosen.
- Transport bottleneck:
 V2O3 anchored on CNT microspheres with macrovoids should improve electron access and electrolyte penetration, yet an over-open structure can reduce density while an overpacked one can still block Zn2+ diffusion.
- Structural bottleneck:
 The active phase has to stay anchored to the conductive scaffold during cycling; otherwise the designed transport network breaks down into unstable domains.
- Process concern:
 Spray drying anchoring has to distribute the active phase and conductive support uniformly without blocking open pathways or creating a fragile low-density product.
