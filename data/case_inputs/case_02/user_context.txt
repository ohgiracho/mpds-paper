[User's Experimental Situation]
- Background:
 We are developing multicomponent chalcogenide microspheres with internal nanovoids as a sodium-ion battery anode and want the architecture to stabilize the active phase before cycle-induced damage accumulates.
- Main bottleneck:
 Na-storage anodes face larger ion size, stronger volume change, and unstable contact, so the structure must buffer strain while preserving active-material accessibility.
- Transport bottleneck:
 Multicomponent chalcogenide microspheres with internal nanovoids should improve sodium-ion accessibility and electronic continuity, yet excessive shell thickness, dead void space, or carbon dilution can reduce practical utilization.
- Structural bottleneck:
 The active phase has to stay uniformly distributed and mechanically retained; otherwise aggregation, shell cracking, or framework collapse erase the architectural benefit.
- Process concern:
 Spray drying with post-treatment has to build the intended sodium-storage microstructure reproducibly rather than generating disconnected domains or fragile secondary particles.
