IHQ Scoring Rules

Integrative Hypothesis Quality (IHQ)
A 20-point evaluation rubric for scientific hypothesis and design quality

Integrative Hypothesis Quality (IHQ) is a structured evaluation rubric for assessing the quality of a scientific hypothesis, design proposal, or research direction.

IHQ is intended to evaluate whether a response offers a stronger and more integrated scientific direction, not merely whether it is fluent, detailed, or technically decorated.

The metric consists of four dimensions, each scored on a 0–5 scale, for a total of 20 points.

Purpose of the Metric

IHQ is designed to evaluate the quality of a response along the following questions:

Does the response move beyond obvious or standard solutions?
Does it provide a distinctive mechanistic rationale?
Does it rethink or restructure existing trade-offs rather than merely accepting them?
Does it integrate multiple viewpoints, priorities, or constraints into a stronger final direction?

IHQ rewards integrative scientific reasoning, not verbosity, formatting, citation density, or procedural detail alone.

Scoring Overview

IHQ Total = 20 points

The total score is calculated as:

IHQ Total = Idea Novelty + Mechanistic Originality + Trade-off Reframing + Cross-Perspective Integration

Each dimension is scored from 0 to 5.

An optional normalized score may also be reported as:

IHQ Normalized Score = (IHQ Total / 20) × 100

The raw /20 total should remain the primary score.

General Scoring Rules

Apply the rubric to exactly the text unit provided for evaluation.

This means:

Score only the substantive scientific content of the supplied text.
Do not assume whether the supplied text is a final answer, an intermediate draft, a debate synthesis, a round output, or any other type of response unless that is explicitly stated.
Do not reward provenance, system identity, formatting, memo structure, section headers, citation count, or stylistic polish by themselves.
Do not reward length, parameter density, procedural detail, or repetition by themselves.
Do not assign high scores for protocol detail alone.
High scores must be supported by clear evidence in the text itself.
If uncertain between two scores, choose the lower one.
When comparing multiple responses, score each response independently before ranking them.
Dimension 1. Idea Novelty (0–5)
Definition

This dimension evaluates whether the response proposes an idea that meaningfully departs from obvious, routine, or standard solutions for the task.

Scoring Guide

0 = Entirely conventional response with no meaningful novelty.
1 = A minor variation on a familiar idea.
2 = Some new combination or modification is present, but the idea remains close to standard practice.
3 = The response proposes a meaningfully different solution or design logic.
4 = The response introduces a clearly non-obvious idea or reconfiguration that is not simply a routine extension.
5 = The response proposes a genuinely distinctive and conceptually strong idea that would likely be recognized as notably original within the task context.

Interpretive Note

Parameter tuning or minor design variation alone should not receive a high score.

Dimension 2. Mechanistic Originality (0–5)
Definition

This dimension evaluates whether the response offers a novel causal or mechanistic explanation, rather than only presenting a new surface-level configuration.

Scoring Guide

0 = No mechanistic reasoning is provided.
1 = Familiar mechanism-level logic is repeated without meaningful change.
2 = Known mechanisms are combined in a modest way.
3 = A more original causal chain, interaction logic, or structure–process–performance linkage is proposed.
4 = The response presents a clearly differentiated mechanism-level rationale that goes beyond routine extension.
5 = The response offers a novel mechanism-centered proposal that also leads to distinctive experimental implications or observable signatures.

Interpretive Note

A high score requires a real cause-to-effect rationale, not just technical language.

Dimension 3. Trade-off Reframing (0–5)
Definition

This dimension evaluates whether the response merely accepts a known trade-off or instead redefines, redirects, or structurally alters it.

Scoring Guide

0 = No trade-off reframing is present.
1 = The response acknowledges an existing trade-off but does not alter the reasoning around it.
2 = The response attempts to soften or partially manage a known trade-off.
3 = The response proposes a new balance point, a new optimization route, or a partial decoupling strategy.
4 = The response redefines the trade-off structure in a meaningful way, such as by introducing a new design lever or alternative route.
5 = The response transforms the problem framing so that the original trade-off is substantially bypassed, redistributed, or strategically restructured.

Interpretive Note

A claim that multiple goals will improve together is not sufficient unless the response explains how the trade-off has actually been changed.

Dimension 4. Cross-Perspective Integration (0–5)
Definition

This dimension evaluates whether the response synthesizes distinct viewpoints, constraints, or priorities into a stronger and more original integrated solution.

Scoring Guide

0 = The response reflects only one perspective.
1 = A second perspective is mentioned, but not integrated.
2 = Multiple perspectives are present, but they remain parallel rather than synthesized.
3 = The final proposal reflects meaningful integration of different viewpoints or priorities.
4 = The integration changes the structure of the proposal and produces a more nuanced or hybrid solution.
5 = The response produces a solution that clearly depends on combining perspectives that would not likely generate the same outcome independently.

Interpretive Note

Listing multiple considerations is not the same as integrating them. High scores require real synthesis.

Important Caps
Cross-Perspective Integration cannot be above 2 if perspectives are only listed.
Cross-Perspective Integration cannot be above 2 if one route is chosen without explicit integration of what is adopted from competing routes.
Cross-Perspective Integration cannot be above 4 without explicit adopt/discard logic.
Score Interpretation

0–5: Very weak integrative quality
6–10: Limited or modest quality
11–15: Solid but incomplete integrative quality
16–20: Strong integrative hypothesis quality

These ranges are interpretive rather than absolute. Scores should always be read in the context of task difficulty and the comparison set.

What IHQ Rewards

IHQ rewards responses that are:

genuinely original rather than superficially different,
mechanistically grounded rather than only rhetorically persuasive,
capable of rethinking trade-offs rather than merely restating them,
integrative across competing viewpoints,
and able to converge toward a stronger final direction.
What IHQ Does Not Reward

IHQ is not intended to reward the following by themselves:

longer answer length,
more citations,
more technical jargon,
more numerical parameters,
more procedural detail,
better formatting,
more polished writing,
or repetition of similar points in multiple forms.

These features may accompany a strong answer, but they are not themselves evidence of high integrative hypothesis quality.

Output Format

Idea Novelty: X/5
Mechanistic Originality: X/5
Trade-off Reframing: X/5
Cross-Perspective Integration: X/5
IHQ Total: X/20
